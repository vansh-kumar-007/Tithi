Frequently Asked Questions

A Tithi (तिथि) is a lunar day in the Hindu calendar defined by each 12° increase in angular separation between the Moon and Sun. Each lunar month has 30 Tithis — 15 in Shukla Paksha (waxing) and 15 in Krishna Paksha (waning). Because lunar orbital speed varies, a Tithi can last roughly 19 to 27 hours.

The Hindu lunar year is about 11 days shorter than the solar Gregorian year, causing lunar dates to drift earlier each year until an intercalary month (Adhika Masa) realigns them approximately every 3 years.

Select "Unknown" for birth time. The calculator analyzes your entire local birth date. If only one Tithi occurred on that day, your birth Tithi is identified with high confidence; if two Tithis occurred, both candidate calendars are generated for you.

Kshaya Tithi (क्षय तिथि) — A skipped Tithi that begins after one sunrise and ends before the next, so it does not prevail at any sunrise. Both adjacent days are noted.

Vriddhi Tithi (वृद्धि तिथि) — A repeated Tithi that spans two consecutive sunrises, offering two valid observance dates.

In the Amanta system (common in South and West India), a lunar month runs from New Moon to New Moon. In the Purnimanta system (common in North India), a lunar month runs from Full Moon to Full Moon — Shukla Paksha shares the same month name in both, while regular Krishna Paksha takes the next month's name in Purnimanta.
